Focus

Research agenda

I conduct applied research in environmental, resource, and public economics with a focus on wildfire smoke, energy use, and health-sector programs. My work quantifies environmental risks and evaluates public interventions to inform evidence-based policy and resource management.

  • Wildfire smoke valuation & risk mitigation
  • Energy consumption & electrification preferences
  • Health economics & program evaluation

Research

Current projects

  • Wildfire Smoke Valuation — regional willingness-to-pay estimates for smoke mitigation across the western United States (Land Economics, in press).
  • Energy & Electrification Readiness — analysis of household electricity and gas consumption and a discrete choice experiment on electrification and energy-efficient technologies.
  • Community Health Worker Program — cost-effectiveness evaluation using linked electronic health records and billing data from UNM Hospital.

Approach

Methods & data

Nonmarket valuation, benefit–cost analysis, and causal inference using survey data and large administrative datasets.

Stated-preference surveys · Difference-in-differences · Event studies · Administrative health data · Energy consumption data

Selected papers & work in progress

  • Consequentiality in Contingent Valuation: Evidence from Western US Smoke Surveys — Revise & Resubmit.
  • Wildfire Smoke Valuation across Western States — Land Economics, in press.

Ongoing extensions

  • Linking survey responses with ZIP-level smoke exposure using satellite-based PM2.5 predictions.
  • Repeated referendum surveys on prescribed fire smoke and perceptions of escape risk.
  • Evaluation of health utilization and expenditure impacts of CHW programs.
