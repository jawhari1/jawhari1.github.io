Environmental Economics · Wildfire Smoke

In Press — Land Economics

Willingness-to-Pay for Wildfire Smoke Mitigation

A regional nonmarket valuation study estimating households' willingness-to-pay (WTP) for wildfire smoke mitigation across 11 Western U.S. states. The study uses a large-scale contingent valuation (CV) survey to elicit preferences for reducing smoke exposure and financing forest fuel treatments.

Methods: Contingent valuation, benefit–cost analysis, interval data regression.
Collaborators: Benjamin A. Jones & Robert P. Berrens (University of New Mexico).

Environmental Economics · Wildfire Smoke

In Progress

Wildfire Smoke Exposure Panel (2006–2023)

Construction of a daily ZIP-code–level wildfire smoke exposure panel using satellite-based PM2.5 predictions from the Stanford ECHO Lab. The panel is merged with contingent valuation survey data to compare self-reported smoke exposure with objectively measured concentrations, and to assess implications for stated WTP and policy design.

Data: smokePM2.5 daily ZCTA predictions (2006–2023); CV survey responses.
Methods: Spatial merge, exposure metric construction, econometric analysis.
Tools: Stata, Python (pandas), ArcGIS Pro.

Environmental Economics · Survey Design

In Progress

New Mexico Contingent Valuation Survey on Wildfire Smoke

Design and administration of a state-level contingent valuation survey eliciting New Mexico households' willingness-to-pay for reducing wildfire smoke exposure. The survey went through five design iterations and is administered via Qualtrics. This project is funded by the New Mexico Research Grant (NMRG) and is IRB approved.

Funding: New Mexico Research Grant (NMRG), UNM-GPSA — $3,000.
Methods: Contingent valuation, referendum elicitation, survey design.
Platform: Qualtrics.

Energy Economics · Electrification

In Progress

Energy Consumption and Electrification Preferences (LADPU)

Analysis of household electricity and natural gas consumption data and implementation of a discrete choice experiment (DCE) to quantify preferences for electrification and energy-efficient technologies. This project is conducted in collaboration with the Los Alamos Department of Public Utilities (LADPU) and aims to inform local energy transition policy.

Collaborators: Yuting Yang (UNM) & Jamal Mamkhezri (NMSU).
Data: Household electricity and gas consumption records; Los Alamos climate data.
Methods: Discrete choice modeling, consumption panel analysis.

Health Economics · Program Evaluation

In Progress

Community Health Worker Program Evaluation (UNMH)

Cost-effectiveness evaluation of the University of New Mexico Hospital's Community Health Worker (CHW) program. The project links electronic health records (EHR) from Cerner with hospital billing data to estimate the program's impact on healthcare utilization and expenditures. Social determinants of health (SDoH) are flagged using ICD-10 Z-codes (Z55–Z65) mapped through the HCUP CCSR classification system.

Funding: UNM Hospital.
Collaborator: Brady Horn (University of New Mexico).
Methods: Difference-in-differences, event study, matched controls.
Data: EHR (Cerner), hospital billing data, HCUP CCSR ICD-10 mapping.
