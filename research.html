Research in Progress

Environmental Economics & Wildfire Smoke

My research in environmental and resource economics focuses on the valuation of wildfire smoke exposure and the design of mitigation policies. I combine stated-preference methods, benefit–cost analysis, and spatial exposure data to quantify economic damages and inform policy-relevant tradeoffs.

  • Smoke exposure linkage: Linking ZIP-code–level wildfire smoke exposure predictions from the Stanford ECHO Lab (satellite-based PM2.5) to survey data to assess how observed exposure relates to preferences for wildfire smoke reduction and acceptance of forest fuel treatments.
  • Prescribed fire preferences: Repeated referendum surveys to study public acceptance of prescribed fire smoke and perceptions of escape risk.

Energy Economics & Electrification Readiness

I study household energy use and preferences for electrification and energy-efficient technologies. This work combines utility consumption data with a discrete choice experiment to inform energy transition policy. This project is conducted in collaboration with Yuting Yang (University of New Mexico) and Jamal Mamkhezri (New Mexico State University).

  • Data: Household electricity and natural gas consumption records.
  • Methods: Discrete choice modeling and applied econometrics.
  • Collaboration: Los Alamos Department of Public Utilities.

Health Economics & Program Evaluation

In health and public economics, I evaluate the cost-effectiveness of social and health interventions using large administrative datasets. My current project studies a Community Health Worker (CHW) program at the University of New Mexico Hospital. This work is conducted in collaboration with Brady Horn (University of New Mexico).

  • Design: Difference-in-differences with matched controls and staggered program entry; event-study analyses.
  • Outcomes: Emergency department visits, inpatient admissions, readmissions, and total healthcare expenditures.
  • Status: Data construction and analysis in progress.
