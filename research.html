Working Papers

  • Consequentiality in Contingent Valuation: Evidence from Western U.S. Smoke Surveys — Revise & Resubmit

    Examines behavioral responses to consequentiality scripts in stated-preference surveys using original data on wildfire smoke exposure across the western United States.

  • Valuing Wildfire Smoke Mitigation across Western States — Land Economics, in press

    Estimates households’ willingness to pay for wildfire smoke mitigation using a large-scale regional contingent valuation survey spanning 11 western states.

Environmental Economics & Wildfire Smoke

My research in environmental and resource economics focuses on the valuation of wildfire smoke exposure and the design of mitigation policies. I combine stated-preference methods, benefit–cost analysis, and spatial exposure data to quantify the economic damages of smoke and assess policy-relevant tradeoffs.

  • Smoke exposure measurement: Construction of ZIP-level exposure measures using satellite-based PM2.5 predictions to link modeled concentrations with survey responses.
  • Prescribed fire preferences: Repeated referendum surveys to study public acceptance of prescribed fire smoke and perceptions of escape risk.

Energy Economics & Electrification Readiness

I study household energy use and preferences for electrification and energy-efficient technologies. This work combines utility consumption data with a discrete choice experiment to inform energy transition policy.

  • Data: Household electricity and natural gas consumption records.
  • Methods: Discrete choice modeling and applied econometrics.
  • Collaboration: Los Alamos Department of Public Utilities.

Health Economics & Program Evaluation

In health and public economics, I evaluate the cost-effectiveness of social and health interventions using large administrative datasets. My current project studies a Community Health Worker (CHW) program at the University of New Mexico Hospital.

  • Design: Difference-in-differences with matched controls and staggered program entry; event-study analyses.
  • Outcomes: Emergency department visits, inpatient admissions, readmissions, and total healthcare expenditures.
  • Status: Analysis in progress; preliminary results indicate reductions in avoidable utilization and evidence of cost offsets.
